How much solar and wind power does Poland curtail?

Volumes are the reductions PSE ordered, by technology and by reason: balancing of the power system or network constraints.

How much

Curtailed energy per month

From January to September 2026 PSE curtailed 1,757 GWh against 1,168 GWh a year earlier (+50%; PV +33%, wind +116%), while the number of hours with orders fell by 24%. Hatched bars before 24 September 2024 are the hourly MW of PSE's announcements (ordered reductions, last published version of each day).

Share of potential output

Curtailed energy divided by actual plus curtailed output. The PV denominator is all PV including rooftop systems that are never curtailed, so the share for farms that can be curtailed is higher.

Trend: last 12 months

Rolling sum over twelve complete months.

Comparison with the regulator

For 2024 the announcements and the report add up to 91% of the PV and 84% of the wind volume that URE reports for TSO orders; a few orders were announced without hourly MW. URE reports: 2023, 2024.

Why: network constraints

Almost all orders are for balancing (a surplus of generation over demand and exports). Orders for network constraints are small and tend to fall at positive prices.

What it is worth

Value of the curtailed energy

Curtailed energy priced at the RCE market price (signed, and with negative prices set to zero), and the sales part of the compensation as PSE describes its method: energy times the imbalance price CEN, zero when CEN is at or below zero. Support schemes (CfD auctions, green certificates) are compensated on top and cannot be estimated from public data.

Value per month

The CEN-based figure exceeds the value at RCE: in curtailment quarter-hours CEN was positive for 46% of the energy, typically higher than RCE, and the floor at zero drops its negative values. CEN above 1,000 PLN/MWh, in 0.7% of the curtailed energy, makes 16% of the estimate.

Compensation rules to verify

Context as read on 26 October 2026 from the sources linked below. The law and PSE's procedures change often; check the current texts before relying on any of this. This is not legal advice.

  • EU law. Regulation (EU) 2019/943, Art. 13: non-market redispatch is allowed only when no market-based alternative is available, all market-based resources have been used, too few facilities are available for competition, or congestion is regular and predictable (para. 3); generation from renewables may be redispatched downwards only if no other alternative exists or other solutions would result in significantly disproportionate costs or severe risks to network security (para. 6); non-market redispatch is subject to financial compensation by the operator that requests it, at least the higher of the additional operating cost and the net revenues from selling the electricity on the day-ahead market that the facility would have earned, including volume-based support, except for producers that accepted a connection agreement without a guarantee of firm delivery (para. 7). Operators report reasons, volumes and measures yearly to the regulator (para. 4).
  • Polish law. The Energy Law (Prawo energetyczne), Art. 9c(7a), lets the TSO order wind and solar units to switch off or reduce output in the cases of Art. 13(3) and on the principles of Art. 13(6) of the Regulation, subject to compensation under Art. 13(7); units without active power control (Art. 9c(7f)) are curtailed for balancing only in a declared threat to security of supply; the yearly report to URE is due by 1 March (Art. 9c(7q)). The rules came with the amendment of 28 July 2023 (Dz.U. 2023 poz. 1681), in force since 7 September 2023; until the grid code (IRiESP) sets detailed rules, compensation is calculated and paid under a contract (Art. 30(5) of that act).
  • PSE's method, as described in the Minister of Energy's reply to interpellation 11689 (24 September 2025): the sales part is the energy not produced times CEN (CRO before 14 June 2024) for each quarter-hour, zero when the price is at or below zero; the support part follows the scheme (the auction CfD settlement, or the certificate price at the first TGE session after the day); prices of sales contracts (PPAs) are not used. Owners apply through PSE's portal (PV since October 2024, wind since May 2025); see conditions of the application, PV and wind pages. Since 24 June 2025 PSE selects units by their average relative redispatch coefficient to spread curtailment more evenly.

Negative prices: ordered and voluntary curtailment

Hours with negative RCE

PSE orders were in force in 94% of the 2966 quarter-hours with a negative RCE price, but only 15% of the quarter-hours with an order had a negative price: 1114 of 3235 GWh were curtailed at negative prices.

Expected output in negative-price quarter-hours

Expected output = PSE's day-ahead plan for PV or wind scaled by the ratio of actual to plan in comparable quarter-hours (same month and hour) with a non-negative price and no order. Output that is neither delivered nor ordered is the unexplained shortfall: market participants switching off on their own, plus forecast error. Wind: in negative-price quarter-hours without an order, output was 17% below the expectation; across all negative-price quarter-hours the unexplained shortfall is about 517 GWh (range 425 to 546 GWh), next to 284 GWh ordered by PSE in the same quarter-hours. PV: the estimate is not identified. In positive-price quarter-hours with an order, actual plus curtailed PV was already 6% above the expectation, so the range (-139 to 274 GWh) cannot separate voluntary curtailment from forecast error and from how PSE counts the ordered volume.
